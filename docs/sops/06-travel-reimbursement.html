Travel Reimbursement

What happens after a trip when the traveler paid for something themselves. The one thing to know before reading anything else: HR submits the claim, not the traveler. The traveler's whole job is to hand over their receipts.

Where: Travel → open the trip → Checklist Submitted to: payables@skyshare.com Owner: HR — the person who arranged the trip No form, no portal: it is an email
You do it in the app The system does it for you You do it outside the app

The blue spine is the app. The dotted-texture box appears on its own when a trip carries a self-booked item. The gold boxes happen outside the app — the claim is an email, and accounting pays on their own schedule.

Part 1

Before you can claim anything

Travel → the trip

  1. Flag what the traveler paid for

    On the trip, mark each item the traveler booked themselves as self-booked, or set its reimbursement to Needed.

    This is what makes the Reimbursement row appear on the checklist at all. A trip with nothing self-booked never shows it — so if the row is missing and you expected it, the flag is what is missing, not the feature.

    In the appPer item

  2. Collect the receipts at the day-after check-in

    The checklist's Day-after check-in item already covers this: get home safely, any questions, every receipt in case a number changed, and did they pay for anything themselves that we owe them back.

    Chase the receipts here rather than later. This is the step that most often stalls a claim, because the traveler has moved on and the paper is in a coat pocket.

    In the appPer trip
Part 2

Submitting it

Email · outside the app

  1. HR emails payables@skyshare.com

    Attach the receipts and give the ordinary trip details: traveler name, the dates, the purpose of the trip, and the expenses.

    There is no form and there is no portal. It is a plain email with the receipts attached. Confirmed with accounting on Aug 1, 2026.

    Outside the appPer trip

  2. HR submits — never the traveler

    The traveler sends their receipts to HR and stops there. They do not email accounting, they do not fill anything in, and they do not need an invoice or an expense form.

    If a traveler asks how to get reimbursed, the whole answer is: send your receipts to HR and we will handle it.

    Outside the app

  3. Set the trip to Sent to accounting

    Open the trip's checklist and move the Reimbursement selector to Sent to accounting. That is the record that the claim left our hands and when.

    In the app
Part 3

Closing the loop

Travel → the trip → Checklist

  1. Tell the traveler it is in

    Move the selector to Traveler told it is submitted. This exists because the most common complaint is silence, not slowness — somebody who knows the claim is in will wait; somebody who hears nothing assumes it was lost.

    In the app

  2. Record that accounting paid it

    Move the selector to Accounting confirmed payment once accounting tells us the money has gone out.

    How they tell us is not settled yet — see the open ends below. Until it is, this stage is set by hand when somebody hears back.

    In the appDepends on accounting

  3. Ask the traveler whether it actually landed

    Move the selector to Traveler confirmed they got it. This is the only stage that closes the trip's reimbursement in the progress count, and it is deliberately the traveler's word rather than ours — accounting sending it and the traveler receiving it are not the same event.

    In the app

Two ends of this loop are still open

How accounting confirms back to us that a reimbursement has actually been sent: not settled. Today somebody has to ask. There is an email thread with accounting about it.

Approval and payment timeline: not specified. Accounting did not give one, so the app does not promise the traveler a date and neither should you.

Both are process questions for accounting, not app problems. Everything up to Sent to accounting is fully defined and works today.

There is no reimbursement invoice, and there is no traveler-facing form

An earlier draft of this process assumed the traveler submitted their own claim, and a fillable invoice document was built for them to complete. That assumption was wrong — HR submits — so the invoice was dropped in full and is not part of this process. Decided by the user, Aug 2026.

If you find a copy of that invoice anywhere, it is stale. Do not send it to a traveler, and do not rebuild an expense form to go with it: the claim is an email from HR with receipts attached, and adding a document in front of it only gives people something else to fill in wrong.

What the app tracks today

One reimbursement state per trip, not four checkboxes: Not started Sent to accounting Traveler told it is submitted Accounting confirmed payment Traveler confirmed they got it.

It is a chain, so it advances one way and the current stage is the whole answer. The row only appears when the trip actually owes somebody — read from the trip's items rather than asked — and only Traveler confirmed they got it counts the reimbursement as done in the trip's progress.

The stages after Sent to accounting are tracked by hand. Nothing in the app talks to accounting, and nothing here is driven by an incoming email yet.
